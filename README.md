# Hand & Foot v1

A playable local first version of the fourth Cards game: React/TypeScript/Vite on port **3004**, with its own authoritative Python/Flask/Socket.IO service on **5104**. Family rules take precedence over the original Bicycle-based investigation.

## Run locally

Prerequisites: Node.js 22.12+, [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/), and the Cards, Hearts, Euchre, and Golf sibling checkouts with their dependencies installed, as for Golf's shared development runner.

```powershell
npm install
uv sync --directory api
npm run dev
```

Open **http://localhost:3004/**. The runner starts the shared local Cards account service, gateway, and separate game services/frontends. Stop it with Ctrl+C. It refuses occupied ports rather than stopping another preview. Development account and game data persist under `cards/.local/split-dev/`; existing Cards users and service credentials are retained. Local mail stays in the file inbox at **http://localhost:3000/__dev/mail/**.

On phones, the game keeps the shared main navigation above an independently scrolling board, with a private-hand drawer fixed to the bottom. Standard 5:7 cards use ten-card hand pages, three open piles per page, and four completed books per page. Swipe left/right in each card area or use its arrows; vertical gestures keep scrolling the board, and swipes do not select cards or change the viewed player accidentally. Player-board and page changes sweep in the navigation direction, and the drawer opens and closes over 180ms. Actual drawn cards glide and flip from the deck into the drawer before it reveals the new hand; reduced motion shows changes immediately. Follow turn sits under the round badge on mobile. Legal destinations occupy a reserved space beside the foot preview, and the selection controls keep a fixed height so selecting cards and browsing a short last page do not move the drawer. These controls use the same staging and server checks as desktop. The development-only **http://localhost:3004/preview/mobile** starts ready to draw and uses local sample cards without creating or changing a saved game.

The hand footer follows the viewed board on desktop and mobile. Other players’ hands show face-down cards and their current count, with their name and HAND/FOOT state in the heading. Each chosen foot’s top card is public and appears in the bottom-right foot preview; remaining hand and foot cards stay private. Team boards include a player selector for inspecting either partner’s cards. Return to your own board to play your hand.

Accepted mobile melds fly from the private hand to their actual pile; destinations on other pages come into view in turn, including newly completed books. Discards travel to the header stack, and a final face-down discard flips before counting begins. The opening hand cascades into the drawer. Individual practice counting keeps its running total, reconciliation piles, and hand visible in the drawer while the relevant board piles come into view. Reduced motion, interruptions, and reconnects settle to the accepted game state without replaying old moves. The mobile preview's game menu includes working sample meld/discard actions and replayable opening and counting scenes.

After the last hand card lands, a mobile foot pickup cascades every returned card from the Foot stack into the drawer, including the card on the next hand page. Controls and Follow turn wait until the cascade finishes. Playing into the foot lets the player keep playing; discarding into it waits for the next turn. The preview menu's **Pick up foot** scene starts with one King left, so either transition can be replayed directly.

Mobile book completion keeps the pile open until its hand cards arrive, gathers every card into one stack, then moves it to its actual location in Completed books. Newly qualified red threes use the same transition. Multiple completions run in order across their pages, and foot pickup waits for the books to land. The preview menu's **Complete a book** scene starts with two Aces ready to finish the five-card Ace pile. Red-three replacement stays on its own discard-and-draw animation instead of starting a normal discard flight.

Use `localhost` consistently for all local sites: host cookies cannot cross between `localhost` and `127.0.0.1`. Browser page requests to Hand & Foot's numeric loopback URL redirect to `localhost` automatically. Local development accounts are separate from production accounts on cards.shmem.dev.

`npm run dev:game` runs just this game's API and frontend. For shared login in that mode, supply the account settings below and point `ACCOUNT_PROXY_TARGET` to the already-running local account service. Without account configuration, guest play remains available. Its original standalone game database remains at `api/.local/handandfoot.sqlite`.

The private development review page at **http://127.0.0.1:3004/preview** provides playable fixtures for full individual boards with eight, nine, or ten open piles plus eight completed books, automatic scoring, a six-player dense table with a long hand, the catch-up draw, and individual tables with 20-, 32-, and 45-card hands that cannot yet meet their opening. These fixture endpoints are enabled by the development runner only and excluded from the production frontend.

Open piles use tighter spacing from eight piles onward, while smaller groups keep their original spacing.

The six-card reminder preview shows a spacious three-pile board. Its six-card pile turns the bottom card sideways; narrow pile columns use a slight tilt. The seventh card must be played from your hand, never picked up from the discard.

The local **http://localhost:3004/preview/opening** rehearsal uses the live Room board renderer, its current player navigation, normal card sizes and ordering, and the real hand dock and top-card foot preview. Play the full opening (it waits for your pile choice), step through or replay eight beats, or expand “Timing, sound & refinement notes” to adjust the sound, speed, table, dealer, and beat durations. A single shuffle cue precedes the selected 0.9-second Packet drops deal, with two squared face-down stacks. All eight beats reserve the full Choose-board and hand-footer height so the outer frame stays fixed. Setup omits duplicate instructions, board pagination, Follow turn, and the empty hand dock; the hand and foot preview appear after choosing. Deal shows only the unlabeled card stacks. Choose retains both face flips, then fades the padded 2px borders and labels in together over 250ms. Arrival keeps its original seat fade/stagger, then hands off after a short settle (scaled to the six seats). The dealer badge starts immediately, lands with its cue, and the dealer beat lasts 1.5 seconds by default. Fixed private snapshots create no room, send no game commands, and preserve your saved room. Preferences and notes stay in the current browser. The route is available only in development.

Live games now use the same opening motion with their actual private piles. The first round starts with Arrive; later rounds start with Dealer and pass the badge from the previous dealer to the next seat. Choose waits for a real decision, and Ready up waits for actual players rather than a timer. Each human acknowledges the completed opening before the server allows the first draw or a bot turn. Rejoining a chosen hand resumes at waiting or Begin, and games already in play continue normally. The local Preview page includes live first-round and next-round opening tables.

Browsing another player’s board preserves the opening’s progress and the selected board across beats. Private pile animations continue while out of view, and Choose waits for a decision without replaying its reveals when you return. The waiting message has animated dots. Begin plays one chime after the discard reveal; ordinary turn changes are silent. The board keeps its reserved height as gameplay begins.

## Included

Bots offer **Easy**, **Medium** (the default), and **Hard** in Create Game. Online hosts can also change the level in the waiting lobby; the choice applies to every bot, persists across rematches, and stays fixed during a match. Existing saved tables use Medium. Historical bot results without a recorded difficulty also count as Medium in personal statistics and the leaderboard, retaining their original completion dates. New completed results save their selected difficulty; online results remain in Multiplayer. Easy plays straightforward melds; Medium compares meld combinations, uses legal discard pickups, and extends natural runs. Hard considers more combinations, saves wilds for useful books, protects clean-book prospects, and weighs visible opponents and end-of-round debt. All levels see only the same private hand and public cards as a human.

For repeatable strategy comparisons, run `uv run --directory api python -m handandfoot_server.benchmark_bots --seeds 50 --start-seed 100`. This plays 150 six-player partnership rounds, rotating Easy, Medium, and Hard through every team on each seeded deal. Results report round wins, mean round scores, foot entries, pickups, and decision time; they are simulated round results, not human playtests or full-match win probabilities. For a complete match, use `uv run --directory api python -m handandfoot_server.simulate --difficulties easy medium hard easy medium hard`.

During your draw phase, the available deck and discard use Golf’s soft backing highlight: a four-second hold, two-second fade, and two-second return. Normal and red-three replacement draws share a 660ms glide and 620ms flip, with a 90ms stagger between cards. Existing hand cards ease into their sorted positions while the actual server-returned cards arrive. The three-card catch-up uses the same motion. New cards stay unselectable until they land; failed requests restore the draw controls, repeated snapshots do not replay the flight, and reduced motion shows the cards immediately. The development-only **http://localhost:3004/preview/draw** rehearsal replays a fixed two- or three-card draw at natural, half, or quarter speed, without sending commands or advancing your saved game.

The development-only **http://localhost:3004/preview/pickup** rehearsal shows discard selection, compatible hand cards and meld targets, staging, and confirmation using the real table renderer. Accepted draft cards slide face up together from their actual hand/discard positions into their server-confirmed melds over 440ms with no stagger. The same motion handles hand-only drafts, existing piles, completed books, and multiple destinations. Hand slots stay reserved during the flight; failed plays stay staged and reduced motion shows the result immediately. Replay, pause, previous/next, direct step selection, and slower pacing support sequence review. Scenarios include pickup, cancellation, an opening below minimum, hand-only plays, and mixed drafts. Recorded views send no game requests or change the saved room. Pacing also controls the pauses between actions.

On desktop individual practice boards, a seven-card book played in one draft lands as an open pile before gathering and moving to the completed-book row. Completed books keep their type: clean books accept matching naturals only, while dirty books accept matching naturals or wilds as long as naturals still outnumber wilds. In a clean book of twos, more twos are natural additions but Jokers are not allowed. Additions preserve the existing cover, count, and bonus until the card lands. Piles are clickable only when they are legal targets for a game action; clicking never expands a separate card inspection. **http://localhost:3004/preview/books** includes whole-book closing and legal additions to clean and dirty books.

A draw scrolls a wide hand only if every incoming card is completely outside the visible horizontal area. If even part of one incoming card is visible, the scroll position stays unchanged. When scrolling is needed, all incoming cards are revealed together with a 16px inset if they fit. Otherwise the higher card in the hand's usual sort order takes priority over the nearer card. Hand edges retain the same inset so the final card is not pressed against the boundary. The development-only **http://localhost:3004/preview/motions?scene=draw** offers both-off-screen and mixed-visibility examples using the live draw renderer.

The round badge opens a Golf-style round record, starting with the latest completed round. Arrows, page dots, and Left/Right/Home/End browse saved round scores and the game total as it stood after each round. Category names and ordering match the round and game result panels. The record uses existing saved scores and includes no card inspection. The local **http://localhost:3004/preview/records** example shows three completed rounds without sending game commands.

Only the host changes start options; everyone can see the current selection. Starting without bots uses the seated count and that count plus one decks; teams must have both partners seated so existing partnerships are preserved. Unseated members stay in a waiting area immediately below the seats; the deal waits for everyone to select a seat. Closing a waiting lobby releases every player's active-table lock without recording a result.

- Two 11-card piles, with just the top and bottom of each visible before choosing a hand. Feet and other hands remain private.

- Individual practice scoring stays on the playing board: gather the owner's foot, automatically cover debt, reconcile physical cards, open broken books, then count surviving books and card values. Partial cards wait above the reserved settlement footer. The completed count advances directly to the end-of-round popup without pile choices, pause, skip, replay, or confirmation controls. Match totals roll up in their existing seats before the next deal or winner reveal.
- Individual and partnership scores settle automatically. Bots settle sides with no present human. Cover removes short-red penalties first and uses unfinished piles before comparing combined bonus losses across all books, flushes, and qualified reds. It preserves the most points while covering as much debt as legally possible, rather than always breaking dirty books before clean books.
- Private per-viewer snapshots; CSRF and origin checks; serialized mutations; persistent action receipts; stale-turn rejection; socket updates with polling recovery; SQLite persistence by default, with PostgreSQL configuration support.
- Online human decisions use Golf's durable 10-minute clock, with a warning at nine minutes. Polling, reconnection, and service restarts preserve the deadline; making a valid choice resets it. ## Service configuration

- `HANDANDFOOT_DATABASE_URL`: defaults to `api/.local/handandfoot.sqlite`; supports a `postgresql+psycopg2://…` URL for an independent database.
- `HANDANDFOOT_SECRET_KEY`: stable secret of at least 32 characters. Local development generates and retains one in `api/.local/session-secret` so sessions survive restarts.
- `HANDANDFOOT_SECURE_COOKIE=1`: required behind production HTTPS.
- `HANDANDFOOT_DEVELOPMENT_FIXTURES=1`: local review fixtures; leave unset outside development.
- `HANDANDFOOT_API_TARGET`: optional Vite proxy target; defaults to `http://127.0.0.1:5104`.
- `VITE_BASE_PATH`: defaults to `/` in development and `/hand-and-foot/` in builds.
- `CARDS_ACCOUNTS_URL`: private account-service HTTP(S) origin. The game uses only the existing v1 private account contract.
- `CARDS_SERVICE_ID=handandfoot-game` and `CARDS_SERVICE_TOKEN`: dedicated private account credential with `sessions.validate` and `profiles.read` scopes. Cards must register the matching principal in `CARDS_INTERNAL_CLIENTS`.
- `CARDS_GAME_ACCOUNT_TOKENS`: JSON object containing a distinct strong `handandfoot` callback credential. Cards must configure `CARDS_GAME_SERVICES.handandfoot` with this game's private origin and that matching callback token. Both credentials are required for account mode; neither belongs in frontend environment variables.
- `CARDS_PUBLIC_URL=https://cards.shmem.dev` in production, with `HANDANDFOOT_SECURE_COOKIE=1`. The account service continues owning its existing `__Host-cards_session` cookie; Hand & Foot retains an independent game/CSRF cookie.
- `ACCOUNT_PROXY_TARGET`: development browser account proxy, default `http://127.0.0.1:5100`. `VITE_ACCOUNT_API_URL` defaults to the same-origin `/api/account`; `VITE_CARDS_HOME_URL` selects the hub and central account-management/recovery links.

Health/readiness: `/health`, `/ready`. API prefix: `/api/hand-and-foot/`. Socket path: `/hand-and-foot/socket.io`; namespace: `/hand-and-foot`. One worker/replica is intentional; distributed room coordination is future work. Database tables are namespaced with schema version `v1`.

## Production releases

The independent `game-vMAJOR.MINOR.PATCH` and `web-vMAJOR.MINOR.PATCH` workflows test and publish immutable images with source/version metadata and release records. Production uses the reviewed Infra Hand & Foot module and Cards release journal. The first activation requires approval of the infrastructure PR, its explicit database migration, and the separately encrypted account credentials. Publishing a tag does not activate production.

Production disables startup schema creation with `HANDANDFOOT_AUTO_CREATE_SCHEMA=0` and requires shared accounts with `HANDANDFOOT_REQUIRE_ACCOUNTS=1`. Run `python -m handandfoot_server.schema upgrade` only with the migration credential during the reviewed maintenance procedure. Runtime credentials cannot create tables or read account tables. `/ready` checks the adopted `handandfoot_001` schema and account configuration; `/health` reports the component, source revision, and version.

The gateway serves `/hand-and-foot/`, forwards `/api/hand-and-foot/*` and `/hand-and-foot/socket.io` to this game, and keeps `/api/account/*` owned by Cards. Public gateways block every `/_internal/*` path. `node scripts/rehearse-release.mjs status.json game-record.json web-record.json [cards-record.json ...]` verifies the exact published images against unchanged peers using disposable PostgreSQL data, including shared login/logout, both socket transports, restart recovery, deletion, deep links, and database isolation. The standalone container rehearsal remains guest-only unless connected to that shared gateway and private account configuration.

The user page follows the sister games with all-time and separate Easy/Medium/Hard/Multiplayer statistics and 42 achievements: sixteen visible goals (including bronze/silver/gold milestones) and twenty-six hidden discoveries. Sole Survivor is credited to the only side to reach a foot by round end; A Leg Up belongs only to the first player to reach their foot in the entire game. Caught Red-Handed is personal to a player with red threes left in hand or foot when the round ends. These three unlock during play or at round end, without waiting for match completion, and keep private card evidence out of public snapshots. It includes all fourteen secrets shared by Euchre, Golf, and Hearts: Photo Finish, Better with Friends, Hot Streak (five wins), Early Bird, Night Owl, Geezer, and eight holiday badges. The private `/api/hand-and-foot/achievements` endpoint derives progress from completed, server-owned account results across devices. Calendar badges use the server's game-start timestamp and each player's saved local UTC offset; older records with no start evidence do not guess dates. Turning enabled music off saves Geezer for the current account through a protected, idempotent POST to that endpoint; offline attempts retry for that account and account deletion removes the unlock.

Games Played counts only finished matches, including both wins and losses. Starting a game or finishing a round does not count; leaving or timing out excludes that player even if a replacement bot later finishes. Starting an unfinished rematch leaves previously finished results intact.

Retained clean/dirty books, runs, and bonus-scoring red threes are recorded after debt settlement; older results still count toward games, wins, scores, and supported round goals, but do not invent missing book counts. Development-only **http://localhost:3004/preview/user** shows sample progress without changing an account or game.

The navigation gear opens Options and Profile. Options uses the sister games’ Turn Indicator controls, with a sound picker, preview, and independent volume saved in this browser. Cards use the standard style; appearance, sound effects, and music use the existing toolbar controls. Profile offers the shared Cards icon library and email updates through `/api/account/profile`, including email verification and fresh account CSRF protection. Cross-device game preferences, hub tiles, production database migrations, release automation, monitoring, backups, and production activation remain separate work. Opening and card-flight choreography are simplified; scoring includes count transitions, payment feedback, and reduced-motion support.
